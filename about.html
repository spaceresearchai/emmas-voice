About Emma's Voice & Related Research

Emma's Voice is a calm, sensory-friendly tablet web app created to help non-verbal autistic children practice playful lip, tongue, and cheek imitation first, and then bridge those oral-motor movements into early spoken words through immediate visual and vocal reinforcement.

👅 Stage 1: Moves Mode

Enlarged, animated lips, tongue, and cheek demonstrations with directional guide arrows. When the tablet camera detects a match, the child receives an instant musical fanfare, joyful voice cheer, and confetti celebration.

💬 Stage 2: Words Mode

High-motivation early speech words (Mama, More, Water, Bubbles, Open…) with syllable-by-syllable animation, parent video recording, and immediate spoken modeling whenever her lips move.

Related Scientific Research & Clinical Systems

Our architecture draws from peer-reviewed research in autism motor imitation, computer-vision serious games, and non-invasive webcam oral-motor biofeedback:

1. Facial Expression & Mouth Imitation in Autism via Computer Vision

Drimalla et al. (2021) — Molecular Autism (PubMed / NIH)

Used automated facial analysis to measure how individuals with autism imitate modeled expressions compared to neurotypical controls. Key finding: autistic participants can accurately imitate modeled facial and mouth movements when explicitly shown, though their movements tend to be slower and benefit from gentle pacing. This directly informed our 0.5× Slow Coach, forgiving hold windows, and customizable sensitivity slider.

2. JEMImE & LIFEisGAME — Serious Games for Autism Facial Imitation

Serious Games & HCI Autism Interventions (JMIR / IEEE / ResearchGate)

JEMImE and LIFEisGAME pioneered webcam-driven serious games where children with ASD copy on-screen expressive avatars while real-time computer vision gauges reward closer approximations—demonstrating improved engagement and imitation accuracy compared to static flashcard drills.

3. CAMI-2D / CAMI-2DNet — Single-Webcam Motor Imitation Assessment

Kennedy Krieger Institute & Nottingham Trent University (arXiv / Clinical Pilots)

Demonstrated that an ordinary 2D front-facing camera (with no depth sensors or wearable markers) can reliably quantify children's motor imitation while copying an on-screen visual model in under a minute.

Search CAMI-2DNet ↗

4. LAOLA & ORAR — MediaPipe + Hybrid Mouth-ROI Tongue Tracking

Speech & Oral-Motor Rehabilitation Research (PolyU / SCITEPRESS / MDPI)

Recent oral-motor therapy platforms (such as LAOLA and ORAR) highlight a core technical limitation: Google MediaPipe Face Mesh provides 478 accurate lip, jaw, and cheek landmarks plus blendshapes, but lacks direct 3D tongue landmarks. To solve this without invasive sensors, modern systems combine MediaPipe with a cropped mouth-cavity vision detector. Emma's Voice uses this exact hybrid design—combining MediaPipe blendshapes (tongueOut, cheekPuff, jawOpen, mouthPucker) with a real-time inner-mouth pink-tongue chroma tracker and 3D cheek-bulge expansion tracking.

Search Oral-Motor Vision Papers ↗

5. Smart-Mirror Biofeedback & Clinical Speech-Language Guidance

Pediatric Speech Sound Disorders & Evidence-Based SLP Practice

Systematic reviews of gamified speech and oral-motor apps show that non-punitive "smart mirror" visual biofeedback (rewarding effort without red-X failure cues) boosts children's joint attention, turn-taking, and willingness to practice at home.

Important Clinical Note for Parents & Therapists: Speech-Language Pathology (SLP) evidence (including ASHA guidance) notes that non-speech oral-motor exercises (like tongue wiggles or cheek puffs) are wonderful for playful imitation, body awareness, and joint attention, but on their own do not replace functional communication or speech-sound practice. That is why Emma's Voice pairs Moves Mode directly alongside Words Mode—and works best as a joyful home companion alongside a licensed Speech-Language Pathologist and your child's preferred AAC / communication system.
